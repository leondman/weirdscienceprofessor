Is a day on Venus really longer than a year?

The Professor says

A day is always far shorter than a year, so a day on Venus cannot possibly be longer than its year.

Fact Check

It can, and it is. NASA's numbers: Venus takes 243 Earth days to spin once on its axis, but only 225 Earth days to orbit the Sun, so the year finishes first. Venus also spins backwards compared with Earth, so its Sun rises in the west and sets in the east. Because the spin is so slow, one sunrise to the next takes about 117 Earth days - roughly four Earth months for a single Venusian morning. Venus has the slowest spin of any planet in the solar system.
