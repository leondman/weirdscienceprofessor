5 Things Your Parents Told You That Are All Wrong (Fact-Checked)

The Professor says

Wet hair causes colds; sugar makes children hyper; knuckle cracking causes arthritis; bulls hate red; the Great Wall is easy to see from space.

Fact Check

Wet hair does not cause a cold: viruses do. A 1995 JAMA meta-analysis found no overall effect of sugar on children's behaviour or cognition, while noting that a small effect or effects in subsets of children cannot be ruled out. Knuckle-cracking studies have not found higher arthritis rates; Donald Unger's one-person experiment was a memorable example, not proof on its own. Movement, not a special hatred of red, draws a bull's attention. NASA says the Great Wall is not visible from the Moon and is difficult or impossible to see unaided from Earth orbit.

Sources

  • Mayo Clinic: Myths about catching a cold
  • Wolraich, Wilson and White, JAMA 1995: Sugar meta-analysis
  • Scientific American: Crack Research
